# Layout catalog

Use the representative patterns below as abstract layout guidance. Do not copy source-project wording, screenshots, names, portraits, customer information, financial figures, or other confidential content.

## 1. Closed-loop framework

Use for capability systems, operating models, or feedback loops.

- Put the answer-first title at the top.
- Draw the primary sequence as 4–6 numbered stages.
- Place the constraint or gap analysis in a narrow side rail.
- Close the loop visually only when the source establishes feedback.

## 2. Dual-driver synthesis

Use when two independent forces converge on one conclusion.

- Give each driver a balanced half-width analytical frame.
- Show evidence inside each half, then converge into one bottom implication.
- Keep the bottom synthesis to one sentence.

## 3. Definition plus taxonomy

Use for a concept definition supported by data types, capability layers, or scope boundaries.

- Put the definition and model boundary in the upper-left.
- Use the main lower-left area for the taxonomy.
- Use a right rail for 2–3 core distinctions or implications.

## 4. Comparison matrix plus findings

Use for technologies, competitors, options, or business models.

- Place the matrix across roughly 70–80% of the slide.
- Put 2–3 numbered findings in the remaining rail.
- Encode capability with consistent symbols or fills and define the legend once.
- Avoid prose-heavy cells; move interpretation to the finding rail.

## 5. Value staircase

Use for maturity, value-chain progression, or product expansion.

- Put the stages on a rising left-to-right staircase.
- Use each stage for current solution, output, and participation level.
- Use the right rail for entry logic and productization implications.
- Ensure the axis meaning is explicit.

## 6. Scenario/problem/solution matrix

Use for industry applications or use-case analysis.

- Group rows by business job or stage.
- Compare need, current problem, and proposed solution across columns.
- Put measurable business outcomes in a separate right rail.
- Keep labels parallel and action-oriented.

## 7. Growth chart plus driver rail

Use for market size, revenue forecast, pipeline, or operating metrics.

- Give the chart most of the canvas.
- Mark phases or inflection points above the plot.
- Put 2–3 numbered drivers in a right rail.
- Show assumptions beneath the chart or in notes, not inside the plot.

## 8. Team and organization

Use for management, advisors, and organization design.

- Put people and credentials in a clean left column.
- Put department composition and operating responsibilities on the right.
- Use authentic, consistently cropped headshots only.
- Emphasize role relevance, not biographies.

## 9. Business-model quadrant

Use when two independent dimensions classify offerings or revenue models.

- Label both axes with high/low direction.
- Put 3–5 offerings in the quadrant with consistent icon treatment.
- Use an arrow only when migration is part of the argument.
- Put the commercial implication in a right-side text area.

## 10. Revenue mix and business-model panels

Use for current-vs-future monetization or signed-contract analysis.

- Use one chart zone for revenue or contract mix.
- Use two or four structured panels for model definitions and migration paths.
- Keep the chart and the panels causally linked through the title.
- Avoid repeating the same narrative in every panel.

## 11. Case study: process, evidence, outcomes

Use for customer cases or product proof.

- Put a numbered operating process on the left.
- Put authentic screenshots or data evidence in the center.
- Put 3–4 measurable outcomes on the right.
- Add context on customer, baseline, period, and measurement method.

## 12. Valuation or decision summary

Use for valuation, recommendation, option choice, or transaction structure.

- Put the result chart or comparison on the left.
- Put method, core assumptions, and risks on the right.
- Put the numerical range in the title.
- Keep the recommendation conditional on the stated assumptions.

## Additional patterns

### Cover

Use a 35–45% white information block on the left and a light silver/lavender hero visual on the right. Keep the title, client/project, confidentiality, date, and brand minimal.

### Agenda and section divider

Use a white text area and a right-side architectural visual. Highlight only the current section; mute the rest.

### Timeline

Use one horizontal spine with 4–8 milestones. Alternate evidence above and below only when it improves readability. Emphasize inflection points with purple, and keep routine milestones gray.

### Dense appendix

Use for full assumptions, model inputs, or diligence evidence that cannot be summarized. Retain the answer-first title, simplify borders, use consistent units, and split across multiple pages before violating minimum type sizes.

### Closing

Use a light data-network or architectural background with one large closing word or decision prompt. Keep contact details and QR codes secondary.
